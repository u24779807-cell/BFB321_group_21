# Impact and sustainability considerations

Smart Spaza addresses a practical operational problem for small food vendors: stock can be forgotten, expiry dates can be missed and reorder decisions may be made without a consistent view of demand or supplier lead time.

## Potential benefits

- **Environmental:** earlier visibility of products approaching expiry can support timely selling, donation where legally and safely permitted, or responsible disposal. Better stock planning may reduce avoidable over-ordering and food waste.
- **Social:** expiry monitoring and product-condition flags can help shop operators identify goods that should not be sold. This can support safer stock-handling decisions, although the software does not replace food-safety procedures or regulatory compliance.
- **Economic:** EOQ and reorder-point estimates provide a more systematic starting point for purchasing decisions. Stock visibility can reduce unnecessary purchases and help vendors prioritize limited working capital.

## Limitations and safeguards

These benefits are potential outcomes, not measured results from a live pilot. They depend on accurate stock counts, correct expiry dates, realistic demand estimates and users acting on alerts. The current prototype does not measure kilograms of food waste avoided, money saved or changes in customer health outcomes. A future pilot should collect baseline and follow-up data on discarded stock, stockouts, inventory value, expiry-related removals and user feedback.

The reorder model is intentionally simple. EOQ may recommend quantities that are unsuitable for perishable goods, constrained storage or a vendor's cash position. Users should consider expiry, supplier minimums, storage capacity and food-safety requirements before placing an order.

## Future sustainability measures

1. Record quantities and reasons for stock removed, including expired, damaged and donated goods.
2. Report expiry-related losses and stockouts over time.
3. Add supplier lead-time history to improve replenishment estimates.
4. Evaluate usability with small vendors and improve low-bandwidth/mobile access.
5. Introduce shop-specific accounts and role-based access before use by multiple independent businesses.
